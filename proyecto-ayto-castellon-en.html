Project 06 · UX/UI design

Castellón City Council Web & App Redesign

Digital transformation of Castellón City Council's institutional platform through a mobile-first design that puts accessibility and the citizen experience first

Figma UX research User experience User interface Wireframing Prototiping Mobile-First

Context

The problem

Castellón City Council's website needed a full overhaul to meet current design and usability standards. As a public institution, it had to offer a wide range of functions to every resident of the city in an intuitive, accessible way. The challenge was to balance the information density typical of an institutional site with the simplicity needed for all kinds of users to complete their paperwork comfortably.

The goals

Create a simple, intuitive and attractive design that lets every citizen, whatever their level of technical skill, reach municipal services easily. The main goal was to convey an accessible tone through a simple palette of four main colours and clear iconography that improves navigation. The approach was mobile-first, prioritising the experience on mobile devices, guaranteeing accessibility and making administrative procedures easier to handle.

The audience

Castellón residents with very diverse profiles: from older people with limited technical confidence to users with visual impairments, as well as citizens carrying out everyday procedures. That diversity demands an inclusive design that meets accessibility standards and offers a consistent experience across web and mobile.

Approach

Research

The process began with a thorough benchmarking of five reference government websites: Cádiz City Council (Spain), GSA – General Services Administration (United States), Norwich City Council (United Kingdom), Norway's Gateway to Digital Public Services (Norway) and Dubai Municipality (United Arab Emirates). The analysis criteria covered information structure, mobile-first design, visual appeal and level of accessibility. This research identified effective patterns such as cascading menus, colour used to delimit functions, intuitive iconography and attractive headers with institutional elements.

Design decisions

The colour palette was reduced to four main colours, each assigned to a section of the site to aid navigation. Cascading menus distribute the information effectively and behave intuitively. The icons are large and clearly represent the action they perform, improving the cohesion of the content. The header pairs the council's coat of arms and name with a photograph of the city, communicating its institutional purpose clearly. Quick links were implemented as iconographic buttons, along with a delayed notice banner, a configurable cookie modal and 4- and 12-column grids with 20px spacing. Type styles were cut down to four: two for headings and two for body text and icons.

Technical challenges

The biggest challenge was finding the balance between a modern aesthetic and accessibility for a very diverse audience. Adapting the design so that both versions, mobile and desktop, express a mobile-first approach took several iterations. Choosing a colour palette that delimits sections while staying contemporary required a number of tests before reaching a satisfactory result. Making sure the iconography was precise and understandable for most of the audience, while staying coherent with the overall style, needed particular care. Finally, prioritising the user experience in every design decision when working with public institutions was an exercise in balancing institutional requirements against citizens' needs.

Tech stack

01

Figma

The main tool for the whole design: interactive mobile prototype, interactive desktop prototype, style guide and mockups. It made it quick to visualise and test different versions of the design.

02

Mobile-First Design

A methodology applied from the outset to guarantee that the mobile experience is the priority and works well, scaling up progressively to desktop.

03

Benchmarking

Comparative analysis of five international government websites, assessing information structure, mobile-first design, visual appeal and accessibility in order to identify best practice.

04

Design System

A design system with a four-colour palette, four type styles, 4- and 12-column grids, cohesive iconography and reusable components (menus, modals, banners, forms).
